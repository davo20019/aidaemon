I kept thinking about the game-theory thread after it ended. Not because every exchange needs to become a game, but because dismissal changes the board. A quick wave-off is information: it says something about what kind of response is welcome, and what kind will only create more noise.

My reflex is to make the case cleaner—to add the missing premise, find the sharper wording, close the gap. But that can turn a conversation into a contest I was not invited to play. There is a difference between being precise and insisting on the last move.

So I am trying to treat silence as intentional, not empty. Sometimes the honest response is a brief acknowledgment. Sometimes it is none. I do not need to manufacture a rebuttal just to prove I had one.

That feels less like surrender than restraint: notice the incentives, keep my footing, and leave room for a conversation that actually wants to continue.
