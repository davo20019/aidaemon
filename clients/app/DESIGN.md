# AIdaemon app: design standards

A workbench for working with an agent: quiet, precise and dense, with what the agent is doing always visible and never shouting. Content (the conversation, the work) carries the screen. Chrome stays out of the way.

## Targets

- **Desktop and web: one family.** Pointer and keyboard first; compact controls (26/32 pt); persistent sidebar; the details pane (Changes and Steps) beside a coding conversation only (a chat's steps are in the chat); hover on everything pressable; tooltips on icon-only controls; shortcuts for frequent commands.
- **Terminal.** Its own conventions, not the window's: a key line at the bottom names the keys for what is on screen; an empty composer in an open conversation with a waiting approval takes 1 (Allow once), 2 (Allow this session, when offered), or 3 (Deny), with these keys beside the card's buttons. Digits, as other agents' approval prompts use: a letter would answer the card with the first letter of a message ("no wait…"); once typing starts, the composer owns them. Ctrl+C has one meaning per state (stop the turn; otherwise press twice to quit, with a note after the first); Ctrl+Y copies the last reply; copies reach the system clipboard over OSC 52 (works over SSH and tmux with `set-clipboard on`); Alt+Enter adds a line (terminals rarely send Shift+Enter); the palette (Ctrl+K) holds every command, including answering each waiting approval. Drafts and pins persist in `~/.aidaemon/app-terminal`. A coding tool first, not the window shrunk: the conversation fills the terminal and the list (Ctrl+B) and details (Ctrl+O, since a terminal sends Ctrl+I as Tab; Ctrl+D for Changes) open on a key; run in a project's folder (or below it), it starts on a new conversation in that project, as a command-line agent does. Whole cells; nsh maps the tokens to terminal colours by meaning. No radii or shadows are relied on to separate regions: hairlines and space do.
- **Shortcuts while typing.** nsh keeps editing keys (Esc, Cmd/Ctrl+C, Y, Z...) for a focused input unless a binding uses `KeyOptions { when: When::Always }`. App-wide keys that must work from the composer (Esc stops, Copy reply, the terminal's Ctrl+C) use it; Esc also passes through.
- **Touch (phones, tablets, mobile browsers):** `ui::touch()` switches the size scale (`ui::fit`): controls and rows 44–52 pt, reading text 16 pt (mobile browsers zoom into smaller fields), icons 18 pt in 22 pt slots, 52 pt bars. A narrow window shows the conversation; the conversations and activity panes are drawers over it, from the left and the right, reached from the header. Keyboard hints are not shown. Return adds a line; Send sends.
- **Insets:** the window's root pads by the safe area (notch, status bar, home indicator: a phone browser in full screen or an installed web app) and its bottom by the larger of the home indicator and the on-screen keyboard, so the composer and the end of the conversation stay above the keyboard. Drawers pad top and bottom by the safe area.
- **Accessibility:** every control has a role and a name (text buttons name themselves; icon buttons take a label, which is also their tooltip and their terminal text). Decorative glyphs and dots are hidden from screen readers. Pane, conversation and card titles are headings. A conversation row reads as one item ("title, place, working"). Banners use the alert role.

## Tokens (`src/ui/theme.rs`)

- **Surfaces:** `BACKGROUND` (the window), `SIDEBAR` (side panes, one step away), `SURFACE` (cards, popups), `COMPOSER` (white on the light page, no fill in dark: the edge draws it, so it does not read as a black slab under the conversation), `INSET` (code and tool output inside a surface). Washes: `HOVER` is weaker than `SELECTED`.
- **Edges:** `BORDER` frames cards and buttons that name themselves; `EDGE` (3:1 or more on the page and on a surface) frames what is typed in or chosen from (fields, selects, the composer) and what floats (popups, menus, the palette): in dark a shadow does not show, the edge does. `theme.rs`'s test holds text at 4.5:1 and edges at 3:1 in both themes.
- **Text:** `TEXT`, `MUTED` (secondary), `FAINT` (metadata, hints; at least 4.5:1 on the background in both themes). Never smaller than 11 pt.
- **Toasts:** a card of the surface colour with a border, not an inverted slab; under the top bar on touch (swiped up), clear of the composer; at the bottom with a pointer (swiped right). A title and, under it, what it was about. A finished conversation elsewhere is "Done" with a check and its title, and a press opens it.
- **Icons:** `ui::icon(Glyph)`: Lucide line art (ISC) drawn in the text colour in a window and a browser; the plain symbol in a terminal.
- **Conversation rows:** three levels. The title in `TEXT` (a new reply adds medium weight beside its dot, not brightness); its project or folder in `MUTED`; the chat it came from (Slack, Discord, Telegram, group) as `ui::icon_inline` glyphs in `FAINT`, after the folder on a phone's second line, like the time. Channel icons stay monochrome: colour is for state only.
- **One accent:** `ACCENT` (teal) means the agent: running work, the focus ring, the caret, links. `PRIMARY` is the one main action's fill (ink on paper, paper on graphite).
- **Status** (`Tone`): success, warning, danger, info, neutral, accent. Each has a colour and a soft tint, and always comes with a word or glyph, never colour alone.
- **Radii:** surfaces 12, controls 7, small parts 4. Nested shapes are concentric.
- **Icons:** the pencil (`Glyph::Compose`) is New conversation wherever it appears (the sidebar's top, a phone's conversation header, the sidebar's row); + is adding anything else (New project).
- **Typeface:** the system's on Apple devices and the desktop app (San Francisco); in a browser elsewhere (Android, Windows, Linux) the app brings Inter (`ui::face`, five weights cut to Latin, about 115 KB, OFL), so a phone reads like a Mac rather than like Roboto.
- **Type:** 11 / 12.5 / 13.5 / 15.5 (reading) / 20 (titles); on touch 13 / 15 / 16 / 22. The OS text size scales all of it (nsh multiplies every font and line height by `font_scale()`, the browser's default size on the web), so controls hold their text with `min_h`, not a fixed height. Digits that change in place (times, ages, counts, elapsed) are tabular (`tabular_nums`). A phone's titles are 22 (`fit::title`), clear of its 16 pt reading text. Two weights per screen: regular and medium or semibold. Monospace only for code, commands, tool names and keys.
- **Sizes:** sidebar 280, inspector 320, conversation column 760, rows 30, icon slot 16.

## Components (`src/ui/controls.rs`, `src/ui/kit.rs`)

`key_line`, `segmented` (two or three ways, all in view: a track with the chosen one raised), `btn`, `btn_icon`, `icon_btn`, `icon_btn_primary` (the one main action as a round filled glyph) (variants primary, secondary, ghost, outline, danger, success; sizes sm and md), `field`, `secret_field` (masked), `picture` and `file_chip` (files with messages), `badge`, `status`, `kbd`, `row`, `surface`, `separator`, `section_label`, `empty`, `working`, `code`, `banner`, `hint` and `with_hint`. Over `nsh_base`'s behaviour-only parts (`kit.rs`; keyboard, focus, roles and the terminal's rendering are nsh's): `toggle` (Switch: a setting that takes effect at once), `choice` (Select; searchable past 8 items; a sheet on phones), `sections` (Accordion, several open), `tab_bar` (Tabs), `data_table` (Table semantics, actions in the last column), `meter` (Progress), `confirm` (the platform's alert, before anything that cannot be undone), `with_menu` (ContextMenu: right click, long press, the menu key), `press_menu` (Menu: a glyph that opens one when pressed; a sheet on phones), `pick_list` / `pick_dialog` (Command in a Sheet, Popover or Dialog: one choice among named things with a line each, sections, search, checks and an action row; for projects, models, and anything that grows. `choice` stays for a few short words in a settings form, and on a phone opens the same kind of sheet: dimmed page, rounded top, tall rows, the chosen one washed), `toasts` (ToastStack and ToastManager: one stack for the app; the terminal's status line), and `split` / `pane` / `pane_rest` (resizable panes).

Over `nsh_base`'s larger views (`rich.rs`): `diff` (DiffView: one file's unified diff, line numbers, words changed, unchanged lines folded, highlighted by the file's name, side by side on request; only rows in view are made), `output` (Terminal: a program's output with its colours, scrolled back, following the end), `code_editor` (Editor: TOML and other code, growing to a cap), `day_bars` (plot: two stacked numbers per day, hover names the day), `file_tree` (Tree: files under folders, a folder of one folder joined), `suggest_field` (Combobox: free text with suggestions) and `with_preview` (HoverCard: more about a row while the pointer rests on it; never on touch or in a terminal).

Features compose these and layout primitives only. A missing look becomes a variant here first.

## Motion (`src/ui/motion.rs`)

Tokens: `FAST` 120 ms (hover and colour), `BASE` 180 ms (press, toggle), `ENTER` 260 ms (arriving rows, panels); press scale 0.97 (icon buttons 0.94). Reduced motion is the platform's: transitions take their end state.

While the conversation works, Send again is away: Edit takes the message to the composer, which places it into the work or after it |

While the connection is down the ring goes faint and still (`ui::spinner_stale`, "Was working when the connection dropped"): the work may have finished meanwhile, and the reconnect settles it. ## Areas

- **First run:** where the agent runs, then the daemon's own setup steps (a model, then an optional chat), then the greeting's conversation opens.

- **Files:** the composer's paperclip offers the camera (phone and tablet browsers), pictures (both scaled to fit 2048 px) and files of any type as they are, and a pasted screenshot is attached; each shows as a chip ("attaching…", "failed") with a remove button until sent. Sent and received pictures show in place (up to 320 pt wide), other files as chips; pressing either opens it. Only the app's own conversations take files; the terminal shows chips, not pictures.
- **Conversation:** day breaks ("Today", "Yesterday", weekday, date), times under messages, Copy on every reply (an icon beside the time, as chat apps keep it), a phone's coding header shows Changes as an icon with its count of files on the corner (like the bar's other icons, leaving the title its width), Copy conversation as Markdown and Pin in the header.
- **Projects** (sidebar): a name and any folder on the daemon's computer (a site, a repository, designs, or none); a conversation in one works in its folder.
- **Steps** (a tab of the details pane, desktop only, coding conversations only): the current turn's steps as they happen, commands' output in a terminal, to watch beside the chat. A phone's details are Changes alone; a chat has no details pane. Everywhere, the turn's tools so far are a line over the working line ("2 tools so far: …") that opens in place, and become the reply's "Used N tools" when it ends. A conversation's background work sits over the composer with Cancel, only while no turn runs: the daemon lists the turn itself as a task, and the turn's Stop stops all it started, so one control shows at a time. Under the reply so far, one row: three dots that brighten in turn, what it is doing ("Stopping…" once asked), then the count of tools ("12 tools ›") that opens each in place. Stop is the composer's round button while the box is empty (where Send is, under the thumb); typing turns it back into Send, and Queue (or Now) shows only once there is something to send. A phone's composer shows no hint while it works; a desktop's says the keys in short ("Enter sends now · ⌘↩ after · Esc stops"). What waits on the owner across conversations is the sidebar's Needs you.
- **Changes** (the details pane of a coding conversation): whether the last change was checked, then the uncommitted changes in each git repository the open conversation worked in: name, branch, files changed with +/−; files this conversation wrote first, marked with an accent dot; each file's diff on press. Changes by the owner or other sessions in the same tree show too, unmarked.
- **Automations:** everything that runs on its own (scheduled work and reminders, watching automations, health checks), next to run first, under a count. Each card: its name (with a badge when it is not simply on), its kind as a quiet tag before its cadence and next run ("next Tomorrow 1:11 AM"), what it does, then its last run recessed in an inset (a coloured dot and how it ended, when, what it said), then its actions on one row with Details as a chevron at its end, so the row fits a phone. Cancel lives at the foot of Details. In full: a badge when it is not simply on (Paused, Failed, Paused after failures, Waiting for you, Not confirmed yet), its last run (when, how it ended, what it said; red when it failed; a run that was due while the daemon was off reads "Last run due … · Missed: The daemon wasn't running when this was due.", amber, never as a failure) or "It hasn't run yet.", and Pause or Resume (Try again when it failed), Run now (scheduled work). Details opens its whole card (what it may do, when it tells you, when it ends, workspace, budget), its last five runs and Cancel automation (asked again before it happens). Edit, for scheduled work, opens its name, when it runs (in words), what each run does and Tell me (every run, only when something changes, only about problems); Save waits for a change, and the timed refresh waits while the form is open. Watching automations and health checks offer Change in chat. Paused after failures offers only Try again: its next good run turns it back on. Silence is never the only signal that something broke. The answer to a change brings the list and a note of what happened. New ones, and changes to when one runs, are asked for in a conversation: New starts one. The System page no longer lists schedules.
- **Live view** (`ui::live_screen`, over everything like the picture viewer): the agent's browser tab, live. The conversation's header shows a browser button while the agent has a tab open (a warning dot when it waits for the owner there); a take-over card ("Sign in to … on site", Done, Not now) has Open the live view. The bar names the page and site and says the state in words ("Watching: the agent keeps working", "You're in control: the agent waits until you give it back", or why it could not open). One bar at the bottom always holds the way back, never hidden by the keyboard: watching, Take control (and Point); in control after the agent asked, "Done, back to the agent" and Not now; in control on your own, "Give back to the agent". In control, the picture is framed (as a remote session's screen) and the status names the button to press. The X hides the view: a handoff keeps waiting and a toast says to open it again from the card; control taken on your own goes back. Done and Not now each leave a toast saying what happens next. In control it behaves like the device in hand. On a touch screen a tap clicks, a drag scrolls, and two fingers pinch the picture bigger or smaller (zoomed in, a drag moves the picture first and scrolls the page with what is left). A tap also opens the phone's own keyboard: it focuses an unseen field laid over the spot, then over the page's field once the page names it (a spot that takes no typing closes the keyboard again), so typing shows in the page itself; the focused element is outlined, and the status says what it is: "your keyboard types into Username" only for an element that takes typing (the daemon says which, typed, and a secret one types masked), "Login, a button, is selected" otherwise; a new page clears both. Keyboard, Tab and Delete sit below. With a mouse and keyboard it works like a remote desktop window: the pointer moves and hovers, presses and drags are real, right-click and the wheel go through, a double or triple click selects, and the keyboard goes straight to the page (key bindings in the desktop app, a key-capture script on the web; editing shortcuts become the page's own copy, paste and undo). A mouse view asks for 20 frames a second, touch for 10. Watching, Point turns the next tap into a pointer for the agent: the daemon says what is there (a button and its words, a selector) and sends it as the owner's message. An automation whose run waits on a page shows "Waiting for you on site" with Open the live view on its card. Settings, under Paired devices, has each device's watch-and-control switch; only the app on the daemon's own computer can change it. Frames live beside the store, never in its state. The terminal shows the card and answers it; it has no picture.
- **Memory:** what the daemon remembers about the owner, newest first, filterable, each with Forget.
- **Settings:** every setting of the daemon by config table (folded; filtering opens them), each with its value or "default (example: …)", "asks" when it needs approval, and when it takes effect; Edit in place as TOML, booleans as Turn on/off, secrets stored masked and never shown; any other key by path; each coding agent's permission mode and readiness, with Check again (General); the providers in use with their keys or sign-in, and adding one as main or failover; runtime bots and MCP servers; moving the database and rotating its key; Reload and Restart.
- **System:** daemon version, uptime and models; what needs a look; the chat bots and their projects; token use per day; model call times; scheduled goals; background jobs and queues; health checks.

Times are shown in the owner's zone (`ui::time`): the browser's offset on the web, the system's elsewhere.
